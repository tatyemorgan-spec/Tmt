Get Your First Local Website Client in 14 Days

A practical, no-fluff path from "I have a site-building tool" to "I have a paying client" — using nothing but this repo's `site-builder` generator, a spreadsheet, and 30 minutes a day.

Module 1

Find 20 Real Prospects

You don't need a huge list — you need 20 businesses that already show clear signs they need a better website. That's a 1–2 hour task, not a week-long research project.

What counts as a good prospect: a real local service business (salon, contractor, cleaner, trainer, café, detailer, tutor) that either has no website, a website that looks 10+ years old, or a Linktree/Instagram-only presence. Businesses that already have a modern site are a waste of your first 20 — you're not trying to win a redesign pitch yet.

Where to find them: walk or drive one commercial street near you and note every business without an obvious modern site; search your city + service type on Google Maps and click through the top 20–30 results, skipping anyone with a site built in the last 2–3 years; ask in local Facebook community groups who people recommend — recommended-but-website- less businesses are gold.

Do not scrape. Don't use bots against Instagram/Facebook/Google to build this list — it risks getting accounts banned and turns this into spam. A spreadsheet you fill in by hand in an hour is faster to start with anyway.

Module 2

Build a Sample Site in Under 30 Minutes

The single highest-converting outreach move in this business is showing up with a finished sample, not a pitch. Nobody has to imagine what you mean by "I'll build you a nice website" — they just see it.

Using this repo's site-builder tool: pick your #1 starred prospect, and fill in a JSON file with whatever real information you can find publicly (business name, services if listed, a couple of real photos from their own Google/Instagram if usable, their real contact info). See ../../site-builder/examples/hair-salon.json for the full schema, or afrodite.json for an example built from nothing but a screenshot.

Only use data you already have permission to use — a screenshot you took, information the business itself published. This is a sample to show them, not a finished product to publish without asking — treat it like a mockup until they say yes.

Run:

node site-builder/generate-site.js --input prospect.json --outdir samples/prospect-name

Open index.html, screenshot it on both desktop and mobile width — those two screenshots are your entire pitch asset.

Module 3

The Outreach Message That Gets Replies

Generic "I build websites, interested?" messages get ignored because they ask the business owner to do the imagining. Your message does the opposite — it shows the finished thing first.

Template (DM or email):
"Hey [name] — I put together a sample homepage for [business] based on what's public on your [Google/Instagram] page, mostly because I thought you deserved a site that matches how good your actual work is. No pressure at all, just wanted to show you: [link/screenshot]. If you like the direction, happy to chat about finishing it out — if not, no worries, have a good one!"

Why this works: it's specific (mentions their actual business), it's low-pressure (explicitly says "no pressure"), and it leads with a finished asset instead of a pitch — the business owner's first reaction is to look at the site, not to evaluate a sales pitch.

Send this as a DM to 5 prospects a day. Expect roughly a 10–25% reply rate from a genuinely custom sample — track it in your spreadsheet from Module 1.

Module 4

Price & Close Without Being Salesy

You're not selling a website — you're selling "more bookings/ calls/customers." Price and talk accordingly.

A simple, defensible starting price structure: $300–$600 one-time build fee (matches the real effort of a custom `site-builder` site: gathering their info, photos, copy, and launching it), plus an optional $25–$50/month for hosting + small update requests if you don't want to hand them a self-serve static site.

When they ask "how much?": don't quote a number cold. Ask 2 questions first: "How many services/pages do you want on it?" and "Do you want me to also handle hosting and small changes going forward, or just hand you the files?" — the answer changes your price and shows them you're scoping, not guessing.

Objection: "that's more than I expected." Don't discount immediately. Ask what they expected and why — often they're comparing to a $0 Linktree, not another custom site. Reframe around the outcome: "totally fair — just so you know what's included, this isn't a template, it's built around your actual services and photos, and I handle hosting so you never touch code."

Module 5

Turn One Client Into Five

Your first client is worth more than their invoice — they're your proof and your referral source.

After launch: ask for a short testimonial (even 1–2 sentences) and permission to use their site as a portfolio example. Most happy clients say yes if you ask within 48 hours of launch, while the excitement is fresh.

The referral ask: "Do you know one or two other business owners who could use something similar? Happy to give you [$50/a free month of hosting] for anyone you send my way who books." A direct incentive turns a vague "sure I'll keep you in mind" into an actual introduction.

Build a portfolio page: once you have 2–3 real sites, build yourself one using the exact same tool — your own site is proof you can walk the walk, and it's where every future outreach message can point.
