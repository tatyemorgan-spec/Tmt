# Call Review: 1 to 15 October

_What my own Close calls say about authority, the why, and the objections that keep coming up. Read it alongside the VSL script (library/vsl-script-freeman.md)._

## The short version

I went through every completed call in Close from 1 to 15 October, about 490 of them. Calls before 1 October have no transcripts, so they're not included. Most were voicemails and quick "call me later"s. Around 45 were real conversations of four minutes or more: first quals, check-ins before Freeman's call, and follow-ups after it.

The structure is there: work, location, funds, credit, the video, booking, Meet link, a check-in. What's missing is depth. Too often I'm ticking the box and moving on, when the lead has just handed me the reason they'll buy.

**The five changes that would make the biggest difference:**

1. **Stop answering the why for them.** "I'm guessing you want to buy your time back and spend it with people you value, is that right?" came up on 10 calls. Every time, the lead just said "yeah". That's my why, not theirs.
2. **Stop accepting the vague answer.** "No target", "not in a hurry", "just want to learn", "freedom". Each one got "makes sense" or "I love that". Each one should get a follow-up.
3. **When they hand me pain, stop.** "Barely surviving." "My back's hurting." "Always stressed." "Don't want to wait till 2028." I said "I get that" and asked about work. That pain is what the call is for.
4. **Zero prices, zero instalments.** I quoted a figure on at least 13 calls and offered to split payments on three. Wilson's wife vetoed the price before Freeman ever spoke. Chris asked for a price, got one, and went with someone else.
5. **I set the next step, not them.** "I'll text you when I'm ready." "I'll phone you next week." "No worries." Every call ends with a day and time that I chose.

## What I already do well (keep it)

- **The one rule and the trailer.** "Not a consultation, not a chit-chat" and "my one rule: watch the video" (Justin, Peter, Stanley, Mary, Daniel E, Shola). Freeman wants this on every call.
- **Why a mentor, not YouTube.** I asked it on Chris, Halimat, Praise and Justin, and got real answers ("free stuff hides the hard parts").
- **Firm voicemails.** "If I don't hear back before the call, we'll have to cancel it" (Ejiro, Peter, Kingsley). That's authority.
- **Moving calls for the partner.** I moved Wilson's call to 6pm so his wife could join. That's the standard.
- **Some brilliant dig questions.** To Daniel Y: "What stopped you starting properly before? Why is now different? I want to be sure that if Freeman puts his time into you, it gets you to your goals." To Matthew: "If you were able to start, what would need to be in place for you to prioritise this?" To Issah: "Why now rather than in a year?" To Praise: "Why not settle with the two extra streams you've got?"
- **Explaining Management.** The £4k minus £2k example works. Leads get it.
- **Handling credit.** I handled Ameer's credit well, and Freeman closed him.
- **Using the cost of waiting.** To Wilson: "I wouldn't want you to keep waiting and it ends up being a year with no progress." To Augustine: "You said January, but getting prepared before the winter months is essential."

## 1. Authority

Authority isn't being pushy. It's the frame you set at the start, a standard you hold, and being willing to let someone go.

### Authority isn't chasing

Gina said this to me on 5 October, and it's worth reading twice:

> I don't like to channel my life around people… when you start sending me messages to remind me, call me to check if I've watched… that's when people lose me. She pushed too hard.

Pressure (reminder after reminder, chasing texts, "just checking in") feels like authority, but it's the opposite. It tells the lead we need them. Real authority:

- **Set the frame once, at the start.** "You applied, my job is to see if you're a fit."
- **Get them to commit to their own time.** "When can you watch it?" Then one check-in at that time. Not five texts.
- **Be willing to let them go.** "If now's not the right time, that's OK. I'd rather you come back when it is."

### Lines to use

When they ask "just tell me how to get started":
> That's exactly what Freeman maps out with you on the call. My job right now is to check you're a fit, because he only takes on people he's sure he can get results for. Five minutes, fair?

When they say "I'll call you":
> I appreciate that. I'd rather lock it in, though, so I'm not chasing you and you're not chasing me. Tuesday at 6 or Wednesday at 10?

When they're half-in (Gina, Moo):
> Honestly, if now's not the right time, that's completely fine. I'd rather you come back when you're ready than force it. What would ready look like for you?

## 2. The why: from tick boxes to pain

### I'm answering for them

On 10 first quals I gave the lead their own why and asked them to agree. Word for word:

> I'm guessing your main goal is to buy your time back and spend it with those who you value most, is that right?

Every time, the answer was "Yeah."

That happened with Gloria, Halimat, Issah, Abbie, Adetitun, Loretta, Ipaye, Justin, Tunrayo and Andrew. None of them said anything I could use. Freeman can't close someone on "yeah".

> **Don't:** Never put words in their mouth. Ask the open question, then shut up and count to five in my head. Silence is where the real answer comes out.

### The pain ladder

Every why answer has three levels. **Don't move on to money until I've heard level 3 in their own words.**

1. **The fact.** "I work 12-hour shifts." "I want extra income." (This is where I usually stop.)
2. **The cost.** "What's that costing you?" "What does that look like day to day?" "Who else does it affect?"
3. **The feeling and the stakes.** "How long have you felt like that?" "How does that make you feel?" "What happens if you're in the same spot this time next year?"

**Example (Daniel E, how it should have gone):**

- **Daniel:** "Working every day and still can't achieve anything tangible. Just barely surviving."
- **Me:** "Barely surviving. What does that actually look like, day to day?"
- **Daniel:** [the real answer: bills, overtime, missing the kids]
- **Me:** "How long has it been like that?"
- **Daniel:** [years]
- **Me:** "And if nothing changes, where are you this time next year?"
- **Me:** "So you want [goal] because you're tired of [pain], and it has to be now because [reason]. Have I got that right?"

Then **write their exact words** in the Close note. Freeman uses them on his call.

### Ask the why early

Dorcas had a ten-minute break, and I spent it on the video and money. If they've only got five minutes, ask the why first and book the rest. Funds and location take thirty seconds on the next call. The why is what decides if there is a next call.

### Price is the big one

Price came up more than anything else, and every time I gave a number it cost us:

- **Wilson.** He asked for the price on the first call, and I gave him a range plus "use a credit card, pay monthly". The next day his wife said they weren't "in a place to pay for mentorship of that much". Freeman never got to speak to her.
- **Chris.** I missed his callback, then gave a price when he asked. He said he had no time for "another introductory call" and went with someone else.
- **Tina.** "Training alone is £3,000, so where do we get the funds for the business?" The number started the objection.
- **Ajaratu.** A good why ("the best time to start was yesterday"), then a price plus "split in two". Now she's waiting until January.

A number on my call gets judged with no value behind it. On Freeman's call it lands after he's built the plan. That's why it's his card, not mine.

## 4. Check-in calls: receptionist or interviewer?

- **Me:** "Did you watch the video?" **Halimat:** "Yeah."
- **Me:** "How did you find it?" **Halimat:** "It was… okay."
- **Me:** "Perfect."

"Okay" is a red flag, not a "perfect". Halimat then joined Freeman's call from a hair salon. The same thing happened with Kenzie ("knew most of it anyway"), Daniel E ("virtually everything stood out") and Dorcas ("so far, so good").

On every check-in, ask at least three of the proof questions from the script (Call 2), and **ask the why again**. "Since we last spoke, why do you still want to do this?" If they can't explain the model back to me or tell me why they're doing it, Freeman's call moves.

## 5. Lines to stop saying

> **Don't:** Any price or range: "maximum 3K", "low four figures", "2.5 to 3k", "6 to 8k with the mentorship". Not even "it's nowhere near 10 grand".

> **Don't:** "He's flexible with payments", "split into two or three", "I'll see if there's wiggle room", "use a credit card and pay monthly".

> **Don't:** "It's only profit with management" or "700 to 2k per property". These are income claims. Use their own goal number instead.

> **Don't:** "Freeman's had people on sponsorship, it may not be an issue." Don't guess on visas. Say it's one for Freeman.

> **Don't:** "Freeman's had 30-odd mentees, 30+ properties in 18 months." Unless Freeman has confirmed it, don't quote it.

> **Don't:** "Sorry if I've disturbed you." "I'm quite busy today." "No worries, no stress" (as the end of the call).

> **Don't:** "I'm guessing your main goal is to buy your time back… is that right?"

## 6. This week's drills

1. **Before every dial:** write down one word from their Q6 answer, and ask about that word.
2. **After every first qual:** write the one-line why in the Close note. "[Name] wants [goal] because [pain], and it has to be now because [reason]." If I can't write it, they don't get a Freeman slot.
3. **Count to five** after every why answer before I speak.
4. **No numbers for a week.** Every time someone asks the price, use the Extra 3 line and tick it off. Count how many still book.
5. **Every call ends with a time I chose.** Never "I'll call you" from them.
6. **Every callback is a Close task with a time.** Zero missed.
7. **One recorded call a day, played back.** Listen for: did I lead the why? Did I accept a vague answer? Did I hear level 3?
