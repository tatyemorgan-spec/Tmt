# The qualifying frame: they applied to us

_Tye, 21 Oct. Use this on every call alongside the VSL flow (library/vsl-script-freeman.md) and the call plan format (library/call-plan-format.md)._

## The mentality

They filled in an application to work with Freeman. My job isn't to convince them Freeman is right for them. My job is to find out if they're right for Freeman: serious about the strategy, serious about having a mentor, and serious about the call.

When I spend the call proving we're a good fit, they sit back and judge us, and they turn up with no intent (or don't turn up). When they have to earn the slot, they value it.

Three things every lead must show before they get a Freeman call:
1. **The strategy:** they understand how it works and can explain it back.
2. **The mentorship:** they want a mentor, not free information, and they're open to investing in themselves if the plan is right.
3. **The call:** they've committed to turning up, on time, prepared, with anyone involved in the decision.

Plus a **real why** in their own words (the why ladder below).

## The five steps (mixed into the VSL flow)

### 1. Set the frame (first 30 seconds)
> "You applied to work with Freeman, so this is a quick chat to see if you're a good fit. He only takes on people he's confident he can get results for, so I'll ask you a few questions, and at the end I'll let you know if I think it makes sense to book you in. Fair?"

Booked calls too: "You've booked in with Freeman. Before he gives you his time, I just need to make sure it's the right fit. Fair?"

### 2. Make them sell themselves
Ask, then stay quiet:

- "What made you fill in the application now, rather than six months ago?"
- "Out of everyone who applies, why should Freeman put his time into you?"
- "What have you already tried on your own? Why didn't it work?"
- "What would you be prepared to give up to make time for this?"

A struggle to answer is information. Don't fill the silence for them.

### 3. Qualify them on the strategy

- "Explain it back to me like I'm a friend. How does [Management / rent-to-rent] actually make money?"
- "Which part do you think you'd find hardest?"
- Can't explain it → "Watch it again tonight and we'll speak tomorrow." The slot waits.

### 4. Qualify them on the mentorship

- "There's loads of free stuff on YouTube. Why do you want a mentor rather than doing it yourself?"
- "What would you want from a mentor that you can't get anywhere else?"
- "If Freeman maps out a plan and it's the right fit, are you in a position to invest in yourself to get started?" No figures, ever. A yes or no to the idea is all I need.
- "Who else has a say in a decision like this?" Someone else → they join the call, or there's no call.
- "Just want to learn" / "just researching" → "Freeman's call isn't a free lesson. It's for people who are ready to start if the plan makes sense. Is that you?"

### 5. Sell the call and get the commitment

- What it is: "It's 40–45 minutes. He'll look at your situation and map out how you'd get to [their goal, their words]. It's not a consultation or a chat."
- What it's worth: "Most people would pay for that hour. He gives it to people he's serious about."
- The commitment: "If we book it, can you commit to being there on time, somewhere quiet, camera on, video watched, and [partner] on the call?" Book only on a clear yes.
- They pick the time and say it back. People show up to times they chose.

### Always: be willing to let them go
> "Honestly, if now's not the right time, that's completely fine. I'd rather you come back when you're ready than book a call you can't commit to."

Mean it. The serious ones push back to get in. The rest were going to no-show anyway.

## Digging into the why

### The ladder: never stop at level 1
1. **The fact:** "I want extra income." (Where I usually stop.)
2. **The cost:** "What's not having it costing you right now?"
3. **The feeling and the stakes:** "How long has it felt like that?" / "What happens if you're in the same spot this time next year?"

Don't move on to money until I've heard level 3 in their words. Then play it back: "So you want [goal] because [pain], and it has to be now because [reason]. Right?" Write their exact words in the Close note for Freeman.

### Rules

- Ask, then count to five in my head. The real answer comes after the silence.
- Never answer for them ("I'm guessing you want to buy your time back?").
- "Why?" on its own can sound rude. Use "What's behind that?", "Tell me more", "Why does that matter to you?", "What would that change?"
- Challenge with curiosity, not judgement. Smile in the voice.

### The vague answers, and where to take them

**"More income" / "extra income"**

- Dig: "How much more a month would make a real difference?"
- Deeper: "What would that money change? What's the first thing you'd do with it?"
- Deeper: "What's it like right now, without it?"
- Challenge: "Most people say more income. What's the real reason for you?"

**"A second income" / "another stream"**

- Dig: "Why a second one? What's wrong with the one you've got?"
- Deeper: "What worries you about relying on one income?"
- Deeper: "Has something happened that made you think about this?"
- Challenge: "You could do overtime or a side job for that. Why property?"

**"Time with family"**

- Dig: "How much time do you get with them now?"
- Deeper: "What are you missing at the moment? Who notices it most?"
- Deeper: "How old are the kids? How long until that time's gone?"
- Challenge: "If this takes a few hours a week at the start, does that work with your family time, or against it?"

**"Financial freedom" / "freedom"**

- Dig: "Free from what, exactly?"
- Deeper: "What does a free day look like for you?"
- Deeper: "What number would mean you're free?"
- Challenge: "Everyone says freedom. What would make you feel you'd actually got there?"

**"Passive income"**

- Dig: "What does passive mean to you?"
- Challenge: "It's a business, so it takes work at the start before it runs. Are you up for that?"
- Deeper: "What would you do with the time once it's running?"

**"Be my own boss" / "leave my job"**

- Dig: "What is it about your job right now?"
- Deeper: "How long have you felt like that?"
- Deeper: "What happens if you're still there in two years?"
- Challenge: "Why haven't you left already? What's held you back?"

**"Security for my kids" / "a better life"**

- Dig: "What does a better life look like for them?"
- Deeper: "What do you want them to have that you didn't?"
- Challenge: "What happens if nothing changes?"

**"Just want to learn" / "researching"**

- Dig: "Learn it so you can do what?"
- Challenge: "People don't invest in a mentor just to learn. What would you want to have done by six months from now?"
- Commitment: "If the plan made sense, would you be ready to start, or is it more for later?"

**"No target" / "not in a hurry"**

- Dig: "What made you apply now, then?"
- Challenge: "If you were in a job interview, you wouldn't say no target. Six months from now, what do you want to have?"

### When they give real pain
"Barely surviving." "Always stressed." "My back hurts." "Stuck in one job." Stop. Don't move on to logistics.

- "Barely surviving. What does that look like day to day?"
- "How long has it been like that?"
- "Who else does it affect?"
- "If nothing changes, where are you this time next year?"

### The why-now chain

- "Why now, rather than in a year?"
- "What's changed recently?"
- "What happens if you wait until January?"
- "You said [ASAP / within 30 days]. What's making it urgent?"

### The why-a-mentor chain

- "Why a mentor rather than doing it yourself?"
- "Have you tried on your own? What happened?"
- "What would a mentor give you that YouTube can't?"
- "Why Freeman, specifically?"
